The new way to work

Meet the Figma agent

An AI agent, such as Claude, connected to your Figma file through Figma's official MCP server. It reads your components and variables, then designs inside the file the way your team would.

The designer gives direction and reviews every screen.

Mobile App v2 · Figma agentillustrative session
> Design a 4-screen onboarding flow with our design system, in light and dark mode
● Reading the Figma file · 42 components, 64 variables
● Plan: welcome → permissions → profile → done
● Building frames with library components and auto layout
● Binding colors and spacing to variables, adding dark mode
● Checking text contrast against WCAG AA, fixing 2 issues
✓ 4 screens and a clickable prototype ready for review

Scroll-driven storytelling

          
Every scroll moves the story one step forward. The visitor sets the pace, background layers move at different speeds for depth, and a product story plays out like a short film.

          
const tl = gsap.timeline({ scrollTrigger: {
  trigger: ".car-story", pin: true,
  scrub: true, snap: 1 / 4 } });

tl.to(".door",   { ... }) // 1 door opens
  .to(".driver", { ... }) // 2 driver gets in
  .to(".gear",   { ... }) // 3 shift to D
  .to(".car",    { ... }); // 4 drive away

          The human side
          
AI is the tool. A UI/UX designer makes it work for people.

          
AI can generate a hundred screens in minutes. Knowing which one your users actually need comes from real-life experience, talking to people, and watching them use a product.

          
Even with the best AI, a great website or product still needs a designer to guide it.

        

        

          

            
What AI does well

            

              
Produces layouts, variations and content fast.

              
Handles repetitive work like resizing and naming.

              
Summarises large amounts of feedback.

              
Turns a finished design into code.

            

          

          

            
What only a designer brings

            

              
Empathy: understands real users' feelings and frustrations.

              
Real-life experience: knows what works outside the screen.

              
Judgment & taste: picks the right idea, not just many ideas.

              
Testing with people: checks the design with real users.

              
Business context: connects design to your goals and brand.
